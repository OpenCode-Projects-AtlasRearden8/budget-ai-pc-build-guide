The Budget AI PC Guide

A friend-level walkthrough for building a used-parts computer that can run local AI, without spending more than you need to.

Welcome. This guide exists because most PC-building advice online assumes you have $2,000, a big desk, and the patience to read through thirty forum tabs. If you want to run AI tools on your own machine — privately, offline, no subscription — you do not need any of that. You need a handful of used parts, a hundred dollars of good sense, and one afternoon.

This is a hardware guide, not a "how to use AI" guide. It covers what the parts are, how much to spend on each, the tradeoffs you'll actually feel (noise, heat, speed), what RAM and VRAM mean in real life, what to do on the first boot, and the honest truth that your old laptop might already be good enough. For using the AI once it's running, see the companion handbook on daily usage — that's a separate project.

What "running AI locally" actually means

A quick orientation before the parts talk, because it shapes every dollar you'll spend.

AI models are big bundles of numbers — think of a very long, very detailed recipe. When you ask a local model a question, your computer reads that recipe over and over, doing a mountain of arithmetic. The two resources that matter are:

  • How fast your machine can do that arithmetic (speed → how quick the answers feel).
  • How much of the recipe fits in the computer's fast memory (capacity → which models can run at all).

Part one is mostly about the graphics card. Part two is about VRAM (the graphics card's own fast memory) and RAM (the computer's main memory) working together. That's the whole economic story of this hobby: speed and capacity cost money, and: - Used equipment sells both at steep discounts. - Off-brand cleverness (offloading part of the model to slower memory) can run models that don't quite fit — slowly.

Everything in this guide is built around buying just enough speed and just enough capacity, second-hand, with a ceiling you set before shopping and refuse to cross.

A word on expectations

A used-parts budget PC will comfortably run small and midsize AI models — the kind that can answer questions, summarize documents, draft letters, and help with code. It will not run the giant frontier models you read about in the news at full size. That's not a failure; those models need racks of hardware costing a house. A well-chosen budget build is also roughly the class of machine small businesses buy prebuilt for thousands — minus the markup, because you chose every part.

Set your budget, read the guide, buy slowly, and enjoy the week before your parts arrive. Building it is honestly the fun part.
